Ask for "receipts from tax season", "photos from the Japan trip" or "the part
of the lecture about kestrels". FilDOS finds them by what they contain. Text,
code docs, PDFs, DOCX, pictures (HEIC included), audio and video are all
indexed **on-device**. Nothing is uploaded, and the index lives in a local
database.

## How search works

Hit `⌘ K` and type a natural-language query. FilDOS searches by meaning and by
words at once, and returns **one ranked list**, never separate "semantic" and
"name" sections:

1. Every indexed passage, picture, video frame and sound clip is scored against
   your query. One model places all of them in the same space.
2. A file counts as a match only if it stands out **clearly** from the rest by
   meaning, or holds at least half your words. Typing an ID, an invoice number
   or an error code searches by words alone.
3. Filename matches anchor the ranking. A file that matches on both meaning and
   words ranks higher, and naming a file kind ("a photo of…", "in a video")
   favours it.

The top result gets a **Best** pill. Less certain hits fold under
**Weaker matches**. If nothing really matches, you get **No matches** instead
of the closest noise.

Scope chips narrow the search to **This folder** or **Everywhere**, or to
**Folders / Docs / Images / Audio / Video / Code / Tag**, and filter by time.
Drop a file on the search box (or choose **Find similar**) to find files like
it.

## Hits know their place

A result points at **where** it matched, not just which file:

- **PDFs** show a **p. 12** pill, and the preview opens at that page with your
  words highlighted.
- **Audio and video** show a **0:25** pill, and the preview's player starts at
  that moment.
- **Text and documents** open scrolled to the first matching word, with every
  match highlighted.
- **Snippets** in the list highlight the words that matched in the passage the
  hit points at.

The same pills appear on the sources of an Ask AI `/find` answer. Clicking one
opens the file right there.

## The preview beside your results

While results are showing, the search panel splits in two: results on the
left, a live preview of the selected one on the right. The best match is
selected first; hover a result or move with ↑ ↓ and the preview follows.
**↵** opens the file and **⌘ ↵** shows it in its folder. The panel keeps one
size whether it's waiting, searching, or previewing, so nothing jumps as
results arrive. In a narrow window the preview steps aside and the list takes
the full width.

## The indexing pipeline

<Frame caption="Files are extracted, chunked, embedded, and stored as vectors — all locally.">
  <img src="/images/embeddings.png" alt="From documents to embeddings" />
</Frame>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Extract">
    Text comes from plain text, Markdown and similar files, DOCX, and PDFs page
    by page (book-size files stay in bounded memory). Extraction runs in a
    separate, watched process: a file that takes too long or eats too much
    memory is stopped without affecting the app. Audio and video are decoded by
    the app's built-in Chromium, and HEIC photos by your operating system.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Chunk">
    Text is split into ~512-token windows that never cross a page. Video
    becomes one frame per shot (a frame every 3 s), and sound becomes 30 s
    clips.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Embed">
    **EmbeddingGemma 2** turns each piece into a vector, on your CPU. Text,
    pictures, frames and sound share one space, so a text query can match a
    photo or a moment in a recording.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Store">
    Vectors are stored in SQLite and searched with brute-force cosine
    similarity over an in-memory cache. That's plenty fast for a personal-scale
    index.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Long files are read in **two passes**. The first 50 pages of a PDF (or 40
passages of a document) are indexed right away, so they're searchable within
minutes. The rest is read once everything else is done.

## The model

FilDOS uses one embedding model, **EmbeddingGemma 2**, in three parts:

| Part | Downloaded | Used for |
|------|------------|----------|
| Text model | During setup | Every search, and all text |
| Vision encoder | During setup | Pictures and video frames |
| Audio encoder | With the first recording | Audio files and video soundtracks |

**Settings → AI & Search** shows each part's download status. Models are cached
in `userData/models`; the embedding worker unloads after two idle minutes and
reloads on the next search.

## The background indexer

The indexer crawls your configured roots (your home directory by default) in the
background. It's designed to stay out of your way:

- **Cost-classed queue.** Removals and text go first, pictures next, PDF/DOCX
  after that, recordings last, so a fresh index is useful in minutes.
- **Duty-cycled** around your activity: near full speed when you're away,
  gentler while you're working, on battery, or when the machine runs hot.
- **Index only at set hours.** Optionally restrict indexing to a time window.
- **Estimates.** **Settings → Indexing** shows how long what's left will take,
  in total and per folder. Estimates use your machine's measured speed, and an
  ETA shows while indexing runs.
- **Skips the noise:**
  - dotfiles, system folders, `node_modules`, build output and caches
  - dataset folders full of machine-named media
  - checked-out **codebases**, which are indexed docs-only
- **Never reads secrets.** `.env` files, keys, certificates and credential files
  are skipped wherever they are.
- **Keeps itself fresh** via a filesystem watcher plus a periodic reconcile.

You can pause, resume, or clear the index at any time from **Settings →
Indexing**. Close the last window and FilDOS can keep indexing from the menu
bar or system tray (**Continue indexing in the background**).

## Keeping things private

Use **Hide from AI** (a context-menu action, or **Settings → Privacy**) to
exclude specific files or folders from indexing. To exclude whole file types,
use **Skip file types** in **Settings → Indexing**. Search and the assistant
never see what you've excluded.
