FilDOS can mount cloud storage into the same window as your local files. Connect
a drive and it appears in the sidebar under **Cloud**, browsable with the same
list, grid, and gallery views you use everywhere else.

## Supported providers

| Provider | Connects with | Delete |
|----------|---------------|--------|
| Google Drive | Sign-in (OAuth) | Moves to the provider's trash |
| Dropbox | Sign-in (OAuth) | Deleted, restorable from Dropbox's deleted files |
| OneDrive | Sign-in (OAuth) | Permanent |
| Amazon S3 | Bucket, region, access key | Permanent |
| IPFS | A write-capable Kubo RPC endpoint (public gateways are read-only) | Permanent |

Google Drive and Dropbox have their own providers. OneDrive, S3, and IPFS run
on [`opendal`](https://opendal.apache.org/), so further storage backends slot in
behind the same interface.

<Warning>
  OpenDAL backends are object stores with no trash, so deleting a file on
  OneDrive, S3, or IPFS from FilDOS removes it permanently.
</Warning>

## Connecting a drive

Click **Connect…** in the sidebar. Google Drive, Dropbox, and OneDrive open
the provider's sign-in page in your browser (OAuth with PKCE); S3 and IPFS ask
for their connection details in a form. Tokens and credentials are encrypted
with your operating system's keyring before they are stored.

For local development you'll need your own OAuth credentials: copy
`.env.example` to `.env` and fill in the client IDs and secrets for the providers
you want to test. See the [Quickstart](/quickstart#optional-connect-a-cloud-drive).

<Note>
  Cloud files are only touched when you browse or open them. FilDOS's on-device
  AI features operate on your local index — connecting a drive doesn't upload
  anything new anywhere.
</Note>
